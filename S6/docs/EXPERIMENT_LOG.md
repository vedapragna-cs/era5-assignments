## E0 — Lesson teardown (COMPLETE, 2026-08-06)

**Question.** What does S6 actually assert, numerically, as opposed to what was said aloud?

**Method.** Extracted the 15 lesson widgets from `S6.webarchive`, read each widget's JS (authoritative
per the source-precedence rule), then drove each in Chromium via Playwright and swept its parameter
space. Harness and raw outputs in `$HOME/shared/S6-extracted/` (`drive_A/B/C.py`, `out_A/B/C.txt`).

**Results (measured).** Full digest at `$HOME/shared/S6-extracted/S6-DIGEST.md`.

- Batch arithmetic confirmed exactly: `global = gpus x micro x accum`, and 8x2x16 @ 8192 =
  2,097,152 token positions per optimizer step. Microbatch slider caps at 8, so 8 GPUs cannot reach
  the ~1M-token target without accumulation — max is 262,144, **4x short**.
- Mixture compiler demand rule is `need = budget x share / (1 - reject)`. **OPUS rejection multiplies
  required supply.** At the widget's own default (120B, balanced, 18%) the Agentic lane is already
  short (11.7B needed vs 10B supply); at 45% rejection all five lanes are short.
- Eval firewall: disabling any **single** gate leaked nothing across 7 candidates; only disabling
  overlap+derived together leaked, and what leaked was a benchmark *paraphrase*. Derived content is
  the thin spot.
- Crash/replay/fork: only ledger mode rewinds the data offset with the checkpoint. In no-ledger mode
  the model returns to step 400 while the data pointer stays at offset 600.
- Token perplexity by lane at mid phase: batch average 8.8 while Indic sits at 35.7 and one ellipsis
  token at 257. **Shard averages hide the signal.**
- Shard usefulness inverts with phase: `reason-proof-077` has the highest OPUS score (91) and is the
  *worst* early shard (score 35, "delay"), then the best at anneal (89).

**Widget defects found (do not reproduce these).**
1. Manifest gate admits a shard at 87/100 with `pii_screen_status` **and** `dedup_status` missing;
   only tokenizer hash, cleaning hash, contamination and license are hard.
2. Manifest `content_hash` is regenerated by `Math.random()` per render — measured: nudging a slider
   and returning to the identical value produced a different hash.
3. Packing simulator runs the same best-fit search for greedy/best-fit/structured (only the pre-sort
   differs), its `concat_chop` never truncates, and at ctx < longest doc it reports **utilization
   105-111% with -48 "unused positions"** (total padding never floored at zero).
4. Padding lab: right/left/crop report byte-identical metrics in all 24 swept cells (the crop branch
   is a no-op loop).
5. Perplexity heatmap's "Pos" grouping view reads a field that does not exist — every token collapses
   into one `undefined` bucket, though the prose requires position-bucket aggregation.
6. Throughput lab: across 648 swept configurations **1** produced any loader wait (peak 2.6% idle).
   Its `readMB` is ~100x under-scaled and `computeMs` is linear in sequence length.

**Consequence for the build.** Each defect became an explicit architecture decision (D2, D3, and the
packing/throughput sections of `ARCHITECTURE.md`).

## E1 — Mixed-lane vs pure-lane shards for the protected floor (PRE-REGISTERED, 2026-08-06)

**Why this experiment exists.** The instructor stated this as an open question in the session and did
not decide it:

> "let us say that 10% is indic. So the question is that do we make a shard in which we have 10% and
> 90% and when we send to OPUS we say we don't care we still going to take it, or we keep the indic
> separately. That is one question that I've not thought about."

**Question.** Does mixing a protected lane into general shards deliver the floor as specified, or does
the delivered share drift with the acceptance rate of the carrier lane?

**Hypothesis.** Pure-lane shards deliver the floor exactly; mixed-lane shards deliver a share that is a
function of carrier acceptance and therefore **overshoots or undershoots the stated floor**. Predicted
direction: mixed-lane **overshoots** when the carrier lane is accepted at above-average rate.

**Prior evidence for the prediction.** S5 measured the course's own always-on protected lane: *stated
design value 8%, measured simulated delivery ~15% of trained tokens*. That is a ~1.9x overshoot from
exactly this coupling — a floor specified in design units, delivered through a carrier.

**Method.** Two arms over an identical plan, corpus, tokenizer, seed and step count:
- **Arm P (pure)** — one lane per shard. Floor injected post-selection as whole Indic batches.
- **Arm M (mixed)** — Indic documents packed into web shards at the floor ratio; OPUS scores the
  combined batch and a protected-floor override forces acceptance.

**Metric.** Delivered Indic token share vs the 12% floor, measured from the consumption ledger over
the demo run. Secondary: variance of the delivered share across stages, and whether the delivered
share can be *audited* from the ledger at all.

**Refuted if.** Arm M delivers the floor within +/-1 percentage point AND its per-stage variance is no
worse than Arm P. In that case mixed-lane shards are simpler and should be preferred.

**Result.** _(appended after execution)_

## E2 — Exactly-once resumption across a changed world size (PRE-REGISTERED, 2026-08-06)

**Question.** Does the loader deliver every planned sample exactly once when the run crashes at one
world size and resumes at another?

**Why it is the load-bearing test.** The course asks only that resume produces the expected next batch
at the same configuration. The production event is a crash at 4 ranks returning on 8, because that is
the machine that was free. A loader whose position is per-rank (`rank r consumed n samples`)
duplicates or drops samples here, and the loss curve does not show it.

**Method.** Run uninterrupted at world_size=4 for N steps, recording the global sample sequence. Then
run the same plan, crash at step N/2, and resume at world_size=8. Compare the union of all ranks'
delivered sample ids.

**Metric.** (a) no sample id delivered twice; (b) no planned sample id missing; (c) concatenated
global order identical to the uninterrupted run.

**Refuted if.** Any duplicate, any gap, or any ordering difference. There is no tolerance band — this
is an exactness claim, not a statistical one.

**Result.** _(appended after execution)_

# Results (appended after execution, 2026-08-06)

## E1 — RESULT: hypothesis supported, but not by the predicted mechanism

Measured (`assignment/submission_artifacts/reports/experiments.json`), 10 steps, identical seed,
corpus, tokenizer and floor:

| arm | Indic share by scheduler lane tag | by actual document content | auditable from ledger | per-stage spread |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **P — pure lanes** | 0.1754 | 0.1754 | **yes** | 0.1511 |
| M — mixed into web shards | 0.1923 | **0.2885** | **no** | 0.1333 |

**Refutation criterion was:** refuted if arm M delivers the floor within ±1pp AND its per-stage
spread is no worse than P. Arm M's spread was in fact *slightly better* (0.1333 vs 0.1511), but it
missed the floor badly on content (28.85% against a 12% floor), so the criterion is not met and the
hypothesis stands.

**The predicted mechanism was wrong, and the real one is worse.** The prediction was that mixed-lane
delivery would drift with carrier acceptance. What actually happens is that the two measurements
*disagree*: the ledger records the carrier lane, so `mixture_lanes` reports 19.23% while the model
really consumed 28.85% Indic — a 50% understatement in the audit trail. Under-delivery would at
least be visible. This is invisible.

**Decision: pure-lane shards.** A floor that cannot be measured from the ledger is not a floor, and
S5's own measurement of the course's always-on protected lane (stated 8%, delivered ~15%) is exactly
what an unauditable floor looks like from the outside.

**Caveat.** Both arms sit above the 12% floor because protected lanes are never rejected while
unprotected lanes lose ~21% to OPUS, so a protected lane's delivered share rises above its planned
share by roughly `1/(1 - unprotected_rejection)`. That is expected behaviour, not floor error, and it
is why the comparison is between arms rather than against the floor alone.

## E2 — RESULT: passed only after a real bug was found and fixed

**The first version of this test was vacuous.** It re-partitioned the delivered sample list across
the new world size and asserted the union equalled the original — which is true by construction. It
reported PASS while the system was broken.

Rewritten to compare two independently produced runs element-wise, it failed immediately:
`order_identical=False`, first divergence at **global sample position 37 of 84**.

**Cause.** On resume, orphaned batches (committed after the checkpoint, consumed by a model state
that no longer existed) were re-served *from the ledger*. That left the planner's pool cursors at
their checkpoint values, so the first newly-planned step drew from the wrong pool position and the
stream silently diverged from an uninterrupted run.

**Fix.** Resume now re-**plans** from the checkpoint, which advances the cursors correctly, and
checks each regenerated batch against the record the crash left behind. This is the stronger claim:
the system regenerates the stream and the ledger is the independent check rather than the source.
Orphaned records are marked superseded (append-only; the ledger never deletes).

**After the fix:** `order_identical=True`, `per_step_identical=True`, 84/84 positions served,
0 duplicate positions, 0 missing, across world size 4 → 8.

**Carry-forward.** This is the single most valuable result in the assignment, and it is only visible
because the test was written so that it could fail. A green suite is not evidence.

# Failures and corrections during the build

Recorded because the corrected versions are the interesting part.

**F1 — Demo never left the final curriculum stage.** With `SCALE_DIVISOR = 3e9` the whole scaled run
was 1,000 tokens while a single step consumes `global_batch × seq_len` = 1,024. Every step after the
first fell through to the last stage, whose band set is `["B5"]`, so the curriculum gate rejected
almost everything: **0 accepted, 39 protected, 29 rejected**. The gate was correct; the scale was
wrong. Fixed to `1e8` (30,000-token run, ~29 steps, every stage gets real steps).

**F2 — OPUS with a fixed score threshold accepted nothing.** Cosine similarity between a candidate
gradient and a proxy direction in ~50k-dimensional parameter space is centred on 0 with σ ≈ 1/√d ≈
0.004. The plan's `accept_threshold = 0.05` is therefore ~11σ out. Replaced with **top-k over the
step's candidate pool**, which is what the session actually describes ("a thousand samples … we
selected 200") and which makes the rejection rate a controllable plan input — necessary because
`need = share / (1 − r)` means the rate has to be chosen, not observed.

**F3 — Protected floors undone by normalisation.** `effective_mixture` raised protected lanes to
their floor and then renormalised the whole vector, scaling them straight back below it. A floor
applied and then silently removed is worse than no floor. Fixed by pinning protected lanes and
rescaling only the unprotected remainder; `floors_survive_normalisation` is now a negative control.

**F4 — Per-batch floors are unexpressible at realistic batch sizes.** With `global_batch = 8` the
smallest expressible share is 12.5%, so a 2% agentic floor became 12.5% and three protected lanes
locked 37.5% of every batch. Measured: **agentic 18.9% against a 2% floor, Indic 26.8% against 12%**.
This is the same shape as S5's measured overshoot of the course's always-on lane (stated 8%,
delivered ~15%) — a floor specified in the wrong units. Fixed with deficit-based enforcement against
cumulative delivery. Agentic now delivers **2.08%**.

**F5 — Shard-level admission destroyed the largest lane.** The manifest reports the worst status
among a shard's members, so one near-duplicate or one unsafe-license document blocked the whole
shard. Every web shard was blocked and the **web lane — 34% of S5's budget — disappeared from the
run**. The symptom was delivered Indic at 35% against a 17% plan, which looked like a floor bug.
Fixed by gating at document level before sealing, with exclusions recorded (3 documents: one
near-duplicate, one benchmark-derived, one unsafe licence).

**F6 — The exactly-once test could not fail.** See E2. The most important correction in the build.

**F7 — `attention_crossings` measured the wrong thing.** It counted pairs a *causal-only* mask would
allow across document boundaries, which is a large positive number by design, and then asserted it
was zero. Split into two quantities: crossings the **real** mask allows (must be 0) and pairs the
segment mask **blocks** (must be > 0, or the zero is vacuous).

**F8 — Late steps ran 10–15s each.** Two O(n²) scans (`_mean_score_for` over all decisions,
`sample_meta_shard_lane` over all samples) plus a full golden-proxy recomputation every step. Cached
the first two; the proxy now refreshes every 4 steps with the cadence recorded in the OPUS summary.
Full run: ~15s.

# Determinism check — RESULT

Two independent full runs, same seed, fresh objects: `tokenizer_hash`, `shard_content_hashes`,
`batch_stream_hash`, `model_weight_hash` and `final_loss` all identical.

Scope is stated rather than implied: **data-stream determinism is claimed exactly**; model weight
equality holds within one machine and thread count (`float64`, fixed reduction order) and is **not**
claimed portable across hardware, because BLAS reduction order varies. The ledger does not depend on
model determinism — which is why replay restores a checkpoint instead of recomputing one.

## An incidental confirmation worth recording

Initial training loss measured **6.2395** with a vocabulary of 512. `ln(512) = 6.2383`. The session
states that an untrained model's loss is `ln(V)` — the uniform-guess entropy over the vocabulary —
and predicts 11.78 for a 131,072-token vocabulary. Our run confirms the relation on an independent
vocabulary size to within 0.002 nats.

## E7 — Learning-signal quality vs training depth (2026-08-07)

**Question.** At what depth does the learning ledger's per-lane perplexity carry any signal, and is
that signal a difficulty ranking?

**Method.** `run_demo.py` at default depth (28 steps, lr 0.35) and `--deep` (5x steps, lr 1.2),
identical corpus, plan, seed and invariants. Measure the ratio of highest to lowest per-lane
geometric-mean perplexity, and compare against the vocabulary floor `exp(ln V) = V = 512`.

**Result.**

| depth | lane ppl spread | all lanes near V? | usable |
|---|---|---|---|
| default | **1.06x** | yes (all > 0.6V) | no |
| `--deep` | **2.39x** | no | yes, with a caveat |

At default depth the model is barely trained, so every lane sits within 6% of the vocabulary floor
and the ordering is noise. The ledger's *structure* is demonstrated at either depth; its *values* are
only interpretable deep.

**And the deep signal is confounded.** Measured token exposure per lane: indic 43%, reasoning 24%,
agentic 12%, web 10%, code 7%, stem 3%, longctx 1%. Indic has both the **most** exposure and the
**lowest** perplexity (166 vs web's 398) — which inverts the expected ordering, because Indic is the
hardest lane in S5's plan and the course widget measures it hardest by a wide margin
(125.9 vs 7.1 for web at the early phase).

The explanation is not that Indic is easy. It is that **lane perplexity here measures training volume,
not intrinsic difficulty**: Indic is protected *and* has the largest pool, so it receives 43% of all
traced tokens and is simply the best-trained lane. A difficulty comparison needs equal exposure or a
per-token-seen normalisation, and this run does none of it.

**Reported, not fixed.** The confound is surfaced in the run log, `evidence.json` and
`v6_recommendations.json` with an explicit statement that no difficulty ranking is claimed. Fixing it
properly means holding exposure constant across lanes, which changes the mixture and would no longer
be executing S5's plan — so the right move is to state the limitation, not to distort the schedule to
produce a prettier number.

**Carry-forward for V6.** Any per-lane perplexity comparison in a real run must control for exposure.
The obvious form is to report perplexity at matched tokens-seen per lane rather than at matched wall
position, which the learning ledger already has the fields to support (`tokens_consumed_when_seen`,
`repeated_pass_number`).

## E8 — Make the anneal reserve auditable (PRE-REGISTERED, 2026-08-07)

**Trigger.** Spec compliance audit against the exact assignment text. It names four OPUS behaviours —
acceptance, rejection, **deferral**, protected-floor override — and states that "a subsystem receives
full credit only when its result is supported by reproducible evidence". The run emits three:
`accepted 154 / rejected 85 / protected 73`, **zero deferred**.

**Cause (measured, not assumed).** Deferral has two code paths, `opus.py:196` `reserved_for_anneal`
and `opus.py:214` `lane_quota_full`. Both are unreachable in this configuration:

- `reserved_for_anneal` — `StreamPlanner.plan_step` calls `pool.draw(allow_reserved=in_anneal)`, so
  outside the anneal stage `LanePool.draw` *skips reserved samples inside the pool loop*
  (`mixture.py:352`). OPUS never sees the candidate, so it cannot defer it.
- `lane_quota_full` — requires a non-protected lane to exhaust its per-step quota within one
  candidate pool. At global batch 8 the pool is too small; never observed.

**The defect is not the missing label.** It is that a withheld document leaves *no record that it was
withheld, or why*. That is the same failure this submission already argues against in E1: the mixed-
lane arm was rejected because the ledger recorded the carrier lane, making the protected lane's real
delivery unauditable. "A floor you cannot audit is not a floor" applies unchanged to a reserve.

**Hypothesis.** Moving the reserve decision from the pool loop into an OPUS decision record makes the
reserve auditable without changing what the run trains on.

**Mechanism.** `LanePool.draw` continues to skip reserved samples (so the *delivered* stream is
untouched) but now reports which ones it skipped. `plan_step` surfaces those as reserve deferrals;
OPUS emits a `deferred / reserved_for_anneal` record for each, **excluded from the scoring pool** so
`cut = round(len(scored) × (1 − rejection_rate))` is unchanged.

**Falsification — this is the point of pre-registering it.** The change is only correct if it is
*purely additive to the audit trail*. It is refuted if any of the following moves:

- any accepted sample id, batch hash, or `global_index` (exactly-once depends on these);
- delivered lane shares, mixture compliance, or the E1 arm-P figures;
- the length-reserve relaxation rate (21.4%) — a separate mechanism that must stay separate;
- the two-independent-run determinism check.

Predicted: all unchanged; `deferred` count > 0; and the deferred sample ids become drawable and
accepted once the anneal stage opens (that second part is the real proof — a deferral that is never
honoured is a rejection wearing a different label).

## E8 — RESULT: hypothesis supported, and it exposed a larger defect than the one it targeted

**The additive claim held.** Every pre-registered falsification criterion passed. Comparing the
delivered stream before and after the change, at the run length that existed when E8 was written:

- all 24 steps' lane compositions and batch hashes **byte-identical** (`diff` on the run-log step
  lines: no output);
- `prepared_samples` 312, `opus_accept_fraction` 0.727564, `packing_utilization` 0.891761,
  `loss_bearing_tokens` 25441 — unchanged;
- E1's non-OPUS fields unchanged; determinism still identical across two independent runs.

Two denominators had to be corrected to keep that true, and both are recorded because both were
real mistakes: `observed_rejection_rate` and `opus_accept_fraction` initially counted deferrals,
which moved the accept fraction 0.7276 → 0.6580. A deferral says *the stage is wrong*, not *the
sample lost*, so counting it as a judged candidate silently redefines a headline number.
`verify_evidence.py` caught the first one independently — the check failed before any human noticed.

**The larger defect.** Proving the deferral was *honoured* required reaching the anneal stage, and
the run never did. Measured: anneal opens at scaled token 29,100 = **step 28.42**, and the demo ran
**24 steps**. So:

- only **four of the plan's five curriculum stages** ever executed;
- the anneal reserve was never released, making every deferral indistinguishable from a permanent
  exclusion — the exact failure this experiment set out to fix, one level up.

This was invisible before E8 because nothing asserted stage coverage. It had been true since F1
fixed the scale divisor: F1 fixed "the run never *leaves* the first stage" and left "the run never
*reaches* the last" standing.

**Correction, and a second bug found while making it.** The main loop now runs 26 steps and the
post-resume advance continues to the plan's end, so run-a executes steps 0–29 and touches all five
stages. The advance was previously a hard-coded `range(4)`; at 30 main steps that ran to step 33 —
**past the plan's 30,000-token budget**, where `stage_for_token` clamps and four out-of-plan batches
were silently attributed to anneal. It is now bounded by `tokens_consumed < run_tokens`.

That overrun was inflating the result: with it, 6/6 reserved documents appeared to be admitted at
anneal. Plan-faithful, the honest number is **1/6**.

**Final measurements.** 27 deferral records over 6 documents, each naming the document, the stage
that withheld it and the reason; one document observed deferred in S1/S3/S4 and then admitted at
anneal. Stage coverage S1 3 / S2 9 / S3 10 / S4 7 / anneal 1 steps.

**Why 1/6 is the right number to report.** S5 budgets anneal at 3% of the run, which at demo scale is
a single step offering 8 sample slots — it cannot draw six documents. Rather than lengthen anneal to
manufacture a prettier count (which would stop the run executing S5's plan), the *release property*
is asserted over every reserved sample as an invariant
(`test_reserve_releases_every_reserved_sample_in_anneal`), and the run reports what it actually
observed. The run shows the release is real; the test shows it is total.

**One non-finding, recorded so it is not rediscovered.** While checking the ledger for duplicate
deliveries after resume, `superseded` looked absent — 0 of 39 records carried the flag. It is not a
flag: supersession is a separate append-only `batches_superseded` event carrying ledger offsets, so
the effective stream is `batches()` minus `superseded_offsets()`. Verified: 6 orphans superseded,
effective run-a stream steps 0–29 with **no duplicates**. The ledger was correct; the check was wrong.

## E9 — Systematic literature sweep on the *systems* side (2026-08-07)

**Why.** The earlier survey (`RESEARCH-ASSESSMENT.md`) targeted the training *plan* — what to train
on, in what order, how often. It barely touched the mechanisms S6 is graded on. Two of our design
choices rested on measurement alone with no external basis: the packing policy set, and a dedup
threshold whose only citation was a vendor blog.

**Method.** Six search areas (packing, training instabilities, dedup, decontamination methodology,
deterministic/elastic dataloaders, provenance standards), then full reads of the five papers that
could change a decision: arXiv:2404.10830, 2309.14322, 2107.02027, 2501.00656, 2504.09844.

### What changed the implementation

**1. Repeated-n-gram screening — OLMo 2 (arXiv:2501.00656 §3.1).** They investigated the batches at
which loss and gradient-norm spikes occurred, found long repeated n-gram runs heavily
over-represented, and measured that filtering them flattens the gradient-norm trace (their Fig. 3).
Rule adopted verbatim: drop documents with a run of 32+ repeats of any 1–13 token span. Their second
intervention — a trainer-side loss mask for anything that reaches the loader — is implemented as
`packing.mask_repeated_ngrams`.

Measured here: planted `web-0008` (60×1-gram run) is flagged and excluded, and its `dedup_status` is
**`unique`** — so no similarity threshold could ever have caught it. A degenerate document is not a
duplicate document. Three new negative controls; one new hard manifest field (`repeat_ngram_status`).

**This closes the submission's one uncontrolled subsystem.** The previous limitation read: *"spike
handling is untestable at this scale — the one part of the system with no negative control behind
it."* The *recovery* half (rollback, skip) remains untestable. The *prevention* half is now fully
testable and tested, because the trigger is a property of a document rather than of a 540B model.

**2. N-gram containment — the published decontamination standard.** GPT-3 filtered on 13-gram
overlap; the open replications use 8–13 token windows. Our overlap gate was shingle-Jaccard, which
measures *global* similarity and therefore cannot see the ordinary shape of contamination: one
benchmark item quoted inside a long host. Added as a sixth independent gate.

Measured on planted `web-0102` (long ordinary article quoting one GSM8K item verbatim): Jaccard
**0.0806** against a 0.12 threshold, no canary, not derived — every gate except containment admits
it, and containment alone blocks it.

**Adding it broke an existing control, correctly.** `firewall_leaks_when_both_gates_off` asserted
that disabling overlap+derived leaks the paraphrase; containment plugged that leak, so the control
failed. Investigating showed `web-0100`, labelled a paraphrase, actually reused **14 words of the
benchmark verbatim** — it was never a pure paraphrase, so the "derived gate is load-bearing" control
had been measuring the wrong thing since it was written. `web-0100` is now a genuine paraphrase
(Jaccard 0.0000, no shared 13-gram), each contamination case isolates exactly one gate, and the
control is renamed to say what it now tests.

### What confirmed a choice rather than changing it

**3. Packing — Ding et al. (arXiv:2404.10830, ICML 2024).** Our `best_fit` is exactly their
Best-Fit-Decreasing over chunk-then-pack. Our own table reproduces their central claim on this
corpus: `concat_chop` truncates **74** documents, `best_fit` truncates **0**, at a cost of one extra
sequence out of 44. They measure the downstream cost of those truncations at 7–13B (+4.7% reading
comprehension, +16.8% context following, +9.2% program synthesis, −58.3% closed-domain
hallucination) and report ≤0.01% padding overhead at billion-document scale — the overhead shrinks
as the packing instance grows, so our figure is an upper bound. Their scale answer is a segment tree,
O(N log L) vs our O(N²) scan; named, not implemented, on the same terms as MinHash-LSH.

The web lane still runs `concat_chop`. That is now a **recorded disagreement with the evidence**
(`reports/v6_recommendations.json.plan_change_recommendations`) rather than an unexamined default:
`packing_policy` belongs to the plan this system executes, and silently improving the plan would
destroy the comparison the run exists to make.

**4. Krell et al. (arXiv:2107.02027)** — the cross-contamination result our segment-isolated masking
implements. Their framing (up to 50% of tokens are padding; 2× speed-up on Wikipedia at seq-len 512)
is why `attention_crossings` must be 0 *and* `blocked_cross_segment_pairs` must be > 0.

**5. Elastic determinism (Mosaic StreamingDataset)** — "samples in the same order regardless of the
number of GPUs, nodes, or CPU workers", demonstrated 8→32→128 GPUs. Independent confirmation that
our world-size-change test targets the right property.

### A correction to what this log already says

E-series entries and `COURSE-NOTES.md` state that spikes are *an interaction, not a data property*,
citing PaLM's ablation. OLMo 2 shows a data property that raises spike probability. Both are right,
and the precise statement is neither:

> Spikes are an optimizer–curvature event — the adaptive edge of stability, Cohen et al. via
> Wortsman et al. (arXiv:2309.14322 §4), where β₂ governs whether the preconditioner or the Hessian
> shrinks — that certain degenerate data reliably triggers. Filter the trigger at curation; still
> never mark a shard harmful merely because it coincided with a spike.

Note Wortsman et al. explicitly scope themselves to *slow-divergence* instabilities and **not** loss
spikes (their footnote 1). Their result that instabilities reproduce in small models at high LR
therefore does **not** transfer to our spike handling, and is not claimed to.

### Not acted on, recorded for V6

- **MegaScale-Data (arXiv:2504.09844, Apr 2026).** Attention's quadratic cost means *packing
  composition* changes per-rank compute: a sequence of a 30- and a 70-token document costs 16% more
  than two 50-token documents. Our throughput model measures loader-vs-step time and does not model
  this, so rank stragglers from packing composition are invisible to us. Real at 3T; not fakeable
  here.
- **Croissant 1.1 (MLCommons, Feb 2026)** adds machine-actionable provenance on PROV-O. Our manifests
  carry the same lineage facts under our own names; emitting Croissant would make them portable. The
  EU AI Act's provenance obligations for high-risk systems begin enforcement **August 2026**, i.e.
  now, which turns this from tidiness into a compliance surface.

## E10 — The deferred improvement items, implemented (2026-08-07)

`TODO.md` carried five improvement items and E9 deferred two more to V6. All seven are now built.
None was required by the assignment; each closes a gap the research passes identified.

### 1. Reference model and excess loss (`tds/reference.py`)

Trains a frozen reference on the golden proxy set — the only corpus defined as "what good looks
like", and already firewalled out of training so it cannot leak — then scores every traced token
with both models. `excess_loss = loss_trained − loss_reference`.

This is the measurement the learning ledger could not make. RHO-1 split high-loss tokens into 26%
learnable and 11% irreducible; **they are identical in a snapshot** and have opposite implications
for V6 collection. Excess loss separates them: high loss with positive excess means the reference
finds it easier and we are behind (worth gradient); high loss with ~zero excess means it is hard for
both and the budget is wasted. Aggregated per lane it is DoReMi's reweighting signal.

**Measured (default depth):** 5,724 tokens scored. Lanes by remaining headroom: agentic (+0.301),
code (+0.211), web (+0.208), stem (+0.187), longctx (+0.099), reasoning (+0.082), **indic (+0.014,
"matched")**.

That last figure is an independent corroboration of E7's exposure confound, arrived at by a
different route: Indic is the only lane the reference does not beat us on, and it is also the lane
receiving 43% of traced tokens. Two unrelated measurements agreeing that Indic is the best-trained
lane — not the easiest one.

Architecture is held identical to the trained model deliberately: excess loss must isolate *data*,
and a different-sized reference would confound capability with domain. RHO-1's own caveat — one
reference narrows toward its domain, arguing for per-lane references — is recorded in the artifact
rather than hidden, because seven references are not affordable on this corpus.

### 2. Token trajectories (`learning.trajectory_report`)

Excess loss compares us to a reference at one moment; a trajectory compares us to ourselves over the
run, which is what actually separates "hard and improving" from "hard and stuck". A token instance is
keyed by `(sample_id, position_in_pack)` and acquires a slope when its sample is re-served.

**Measured:** 1,557 instances with a slope, 917 seen once (reported separately — a single
observation has no trajectory, and bucketing it anyway is how a level gets mistaken for a trend).
Mean loss delta across repeated exposures **−0.046**, i.e. the slope is being measured, not assumed.

**Explicitly not comparable to RHO-1's distribution.** At this depth the model sits near the
vocabulary floor, so H→H is inflated by training depth rather than by aleatoric content. The
mechanism is what is demonstrated.

### 3. Spike attribution (`tds/spike.py`) — and it found something

Spikes are recorded as `run_event` rows in `ledgers/run_events.jsonl`, never as a shard property.
Any shard exposed in a spike window has its verdict **withheld** (`unattributed`) rather than
guessed, and the withholding is resolvable by PaLM's own ablation — restart from an earlier
checkpoint, replay the identical recorded batches, see whether the spike recurs. That experiment is
only possible because replay is byte-exact; it is the payoff for the whole deterministic-stream
design.

**The detector fired on the real run.** Step 29, gradient norm, z = 6.20 (0.4063 against a 0.2127
trailing baseline). The ablation replayed it from `ckpt_00018` and it **reproduced**. Step 29 is the
**anneal seam** — the first step of the final stage, where lane composition changes sharply.

That distinction is the finding. "Reproducible" would ordinarily implicate the batch content, but a
reproducible jump *at a stage transition* means the mixture changed, not that the documents are bad.
The verdict is therefore `seam_composition`, and the consequence recorded is "the fix is seam
blending, not removing data". Five shard verdicts were withheld rather than marked harmful.

Without this, the learning ledger would have written `harmful` onto five perfectly good shards
because they happened to be in the batch where the curriculum changed — a permanent, wrong label
shipped to the next cohort with nothing downstream able to detect it.

### 4. Ceiling re-parameterised to per-lane `r` (`mixture.feasibility`)

`epochs` is now `repetition_factor_r`, with a per-lane `ceiling_applied` and `ceiling_source`. The
level is **unchanged at 4.0** and the artifact says why: arXiv:2605.12715 measures optimal `r` and
optimal target weight both *falling* with model size (9.5% @101M → 1.9% @539M) and states that
extrapolation beyond ~900M is open, while we plan ~30B. Re-shaping is free; raising is an
extrapolation the evidence does not support.

### 5. The dedup basis question — settled, and it changes a verdict

**Answer: S5's supply figures are pre-dedup, and S5 says so.** `S5/SUPPLY-AUDIT.md` §7 "Still not
obtained" lists the Sangraha-Verified/IndicCorpV2 dedup rate and the judgment-corpus dedup rate as
unmeasured. A documented gap, not an oversight.

So every `within_ceiling` verdict is computed on an upper bound of real supply. Rather than guess a
rate, `MixturePlan.dedup_sensitivity()` computes the break-even `d_max = 1 − demand/(supply × ceiling)`:

| lane | r at published supply | max tolerable dedup | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| agentic | 126.59 | −3064.7% | already breached |
| indic | 4.03 | **−0.8%** | already breached |
| reasoning | 3.99 | **+0.3%** | **fragile** |
| stem | 2.99 | +25.3% | robust |
| code | 0.79 | +80.2% | robust |
| longctx | 0.79 | +80.2% | robust |
| web | 0.26 | +93.5% | robust |

**`reasoning` reads `[OK]` in the feasibility table and has a margin of three tenths of one percent.**
A binary ceiling verdict hides that completely. Three lanes, not two, cannot be signed off until the
dedup rate for their sources is measured — and that measurement is the cheapest unblocking work in
the entire plan.

### 6. Per-rank compute skew (`throughput.rank_compute_skew`)

MegaScale-Data (arXiv:2504.09844): attention is quadratic in segment length, so a 30+70 sequence
costs ~16% more than 50+50 **at identical token counts**.

**Measured on our own loader: tokens differ by 0.0% across 4 ranks while attention cost differs by
46.2%** (straggler rank 0). Our partition is `global_index % world_size`, which balances token count
and says nothing about composition — so a token-counting monitor would report perfect balance while
the slowest rank sets the step time.

Measured, not fixed, and the reason is recorded: a composition-aware partition stops the rank
assignment being a pure function of the global index, which is exactly the property that makes
resumption across a changed world size exact. That is a real design tension, not an oversight.

### 7. Croissant provenance (`tds/croissant.py`)

Emits `manifests/croissant.json` — every admitted shard as a `cr:FileObject` with `prov:wasGeneratedBy`
lineage naming the tokenizer and cleaning-pipeline hashes, and the quality gates as structured
fields. Conformance is claimed as **UNVALIDATED** in the artifact itself, because the MLCommons
validator needs network; running it is the named next step rather than an assertion.

### Two leads read, and one favourable non-finding

**arXiv:2402.13991** confirms two of our choices independently. Intra-document causal masking — our
segment-isolated attention — achieves the lowest perplexity of every strategy they test (−1.3 PPL at
2K, −2.0 at 8K on GitHub vs UniChunk), and they note it is *not* common in open-source frameworks
because it is assumed to cost efficiency. Their UniChunk (same-source packing, avoiding mixing web
and code) is essentially our pure-lane shards, which E1 chose for **auditability** — this paper says
it also helps quality, a justification we did not know we had. Their BM25Chunk (retrieval-based
related-document packing) beats it further: +11.6% in-context learning, +9.8% knowledge
memorisation, +7.2% context utilisation. That is a concrete V6 upgrade we do not implement.

**arXiv:2407.16607** shows a BPE merge list leaks its training mixture — they recover GPT-4o at 39%
non-English, Llama 3 at 48% multilingual, GPT-3.5 and Claude at ~60% code. Checked our exposure:
`tokenizer.to_record()` publishes `version`, `size`, `n_merges`, `hash` and specials — **not the merge
list** — so our manifests do not leak the mixture. That was accidental rather than designed, and is
now recorded as a property to preserve. The same technique is also a verification tool: it can audit
whether a supplied tokenizer matches the mixture its vendor claims.

## E11 — Comparison against a sibling implementation, and what it changed (2026-08-07)

A second implementation of this assignment exists at `vedapragna-cs/era-v5-s6-data-ledger`, built
independently on another box. Cloned it and compared against the spec's requirements rather than the
prose. Three things were worth taking; one of them was a genuine defect in ours.

### The defect: our attention mask was asserted, never enforced

Their model is a PyTorch transformer that applies a per-sequence `(B, L, L)` boolean mask via
`scores.masked_fill(~attn_mask, -inf)`. Ours was a **bigram MLP with no attention mechanism at all** —
embedding → tanh → logits, predicting token *t+1* from token *t*.

So `attention_crossings == 0` and `blocked_cross_segment_pairs > 0` were properties of a *mask array*.
Correct, checked, non-vacuous as array claims — and never exercised, because no mechanism existed
that could have leaked across a document boundary. Against the 150-point packing/masks section and
Step 3's "verify the behaviour was not simulated", that is a fair hit, and the file's own docstring
had rationalised it: *"a transformer would add compute without changing a single ledger property."*
That sentence was wrong about the one property this submission argues hardest for.

**Fixed.** `TinyLM` now has single-head masked self-attention with a residual, and hand-written
backward pass (no autograd, so the gradient chain is auditable line by line). The mask is
`causal AND same-segment`, the identical condition `packing.py` validates structurally.

**Verified two ways.**

1. *Gradients* — checked against central finite differences on the largest-magnitude entry of all
   nine parameter tensors: **max relative error 3.2e-06**, max absolute error 3.9e-10.
   (`test_hand_written_gradients_match_finite_differences`.)
2. *Isolation, by perturbation rather than inspection* — rewrite document 1 of a packed window and
   document 2's per-token losses are **bit-identical**; merge the segment ids and the same rewrite
   moves them by **1.7e-03**.

The second row is the whole point. Without it the first would also pass on a model that simply never
looks across the boundary — which is exactly what our previous model was. The control is what makes
the isolation attributable to the mask.

*A wrong turn worth recording:* the first version of that experiment rewrote the **later** document
and checked the earlier one. It passed with masking and also passed without it, because attention is
causal — a later document can never influence an earlier one whatever the mask says. The test was
measuring nothing. The leak direction is earlier → later.

### Taken: the checkpoint torn-write drill

Theirs runs a corruption drill over saved checkpoints. Ours had integrity *fields*
(`checkpoint_hash`, `model_weight_hash`) but **nothing verified them** — `restore()` simply loaded.

Now `verify_checkpoint()` recomputes both and `restore()` **raises** rather than warning, with six
induced flavours all required to be caught: weight bit-flip, torn/truncated weight blob, loader
cursor moved, ledger offset moved, step counter moved, tokenizer identity swapped. Plus a separate
control that `restore()` genuinely refuses.

The two hashes fail differently and the split matters: a corrupted **loader cursor** leaves the model
perfectly valid and resumes from the wrong position in the data stream — the failure no loss curve
reveals.

### Taken: per-curriculum-stage throughput

A single run-total averages away the variation a mixture plan is made of. Measured per stage:

| stage | steps | packing utilization | useful tokens/s |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1_seed | 3 | 0.846 | 7,536.6 |
| S2_general | 9 | 0.964 | 11,834.9 |
| S3_reasoning | 10 | 0.919 | 10,109.1 |
| S4_longctx | 9 | 0.868 | 6,944.5 |
| anneal | 1 | 0.771 | 11,574.2 |

Long-context is the expensive stage per useful token, which a run total hid entirely.

### Not taken, and why

- **Their scale** (122 shards, vocab 1024) is larger than ours (7 shards, vocab 512). Ours is authored
  rather than generated so every hash in the evidence bundle is stable and nothing downloads.
  Scaling up would move every measured number for presentational benefit only.
- **Their ledger truncates** rows after the last checkpoint on resume. Defensible — those tokens were
  never really consumed — but it destroys the record that the batches were once served. Ours
  supersedes and keeps history, which is the more auditable of the two.
- **Their dependency on torch/tokenizers.** Ours needs only numpy, so the "one command" in the
  submission is genuinely one command and needs no network. Given grading Step 1 runs the submitted
  command, that is worth keeping.

### Where they remain ahead

Scale, and a real multi-head transformer rather than our single head. Neither changes a ledger
property, which is the claim their README and ours both rest on — but it is worth stating plainly
rather than pretending the comparison was one-sided.

## E12 — Second comparison pass: four more findings, one of them a defect (2026-08-07)

E11 covered the model. This pass read the sibling implementation's remaining modules
(`registry.py`, `events.py`, `advisory.py`, `perf.py`, `stream.py`) against ours.

### Defect in ours: five hardcoded `[PASS]` lines

Their `events.py` states the principle exactly: *"a helper that lets a caller write
`emit_pass('thing_worked')` unconditionally is a helper for producing evidence that isn't
evidence."*

Ours had six unconditional PASS emissions — `tokenizer_hash_verified`, `manifests_validated`,
`batches_packed_and_committed`, `opus_decisions_recorded`, `audit_completed`, and
`checkpoint_saved`. Each printed PASS with **no condition evaluated**.

This matters more than it looks. `evidence.json` was already rigorous — `tds/evidence.py` contains
no literal `True` and every requirement carries the values it compared. But `run.log` is a separate
required deliverable that a grader reads directly, and the spec says plainly: *"Hardcoded evidence
will not be accepted."* Six of its PASS lines were exactly that.

All six now derive their verdict from an observed value, e.g.:

```
[PASS] tokenizer_hash_verified (7 shard manifests carry exactly one tokenizer hash, equal to the
       frozen tokenizer's own)
[PASS] batches_packed_and_committed (26 batch records on run-a for 26 steps trained)
[PASS] opus_decisions_recorded (225 decisions, 225 carrying an explicit reason)
[PASS] checkpoint_saved ckpt_00000 @ ledger_offset=0 integrity=verified
```

`checkpoint_saved` is the sharpest of them: it now runs `verify_checkpoint()` and can say FAIL.

### Taken: the shard-perplexity reuse advisory

Their `advisory.py` turns per-shard loss into a V6 *placement decision* using a ratio against the
run-wide median (cutoff 1.2, symmetric). Ours classified shards `useful/neutral/harmful` from loss
delta, which is a diagnostic rather than a decision, and used no corpus-relative baseline.

Implemented as `LearningLedger.reuse_advisory`. A ratio rather than an absolute, because absolute
perplexity depends on vocabulary size and training depth. It composes with the two measurements
added in E10: the ratio says a shard is *hard*, and only excess loss says whether hard means
learnable or irreducible — the report says so and cross-references. It also inherits spike
attribution: a shard whose verdict is withheld returns `withheld_pending_spike_attribution` rather
than a perplexity verdict, because a shard in a spiking batch has no trustworthy signal either.

### Taken: three throughput rates, not two

Their `perf.py` separates **window tokens/s** (padding counted as work) from **real tokens/s**
(padding excluded) from **loss-bearing tokens/s** (also excluding masked context). We reported the
last two. The first is the number that flatters a run, and naming it is how you stop it being quoted.

### Taken (partly): stage eligibility declared on the manifest

Their `registry.py` cuts shards per (lane, split, **band**) and derives stage eligibility from the
shard, so ineligible data is never drawn. Measured on our run: **93 of 323 OPUS decisions are
`stage_mismatch`** — candidates the planner drew and the stage gate discarded.

**We deliberately did not adopt the mechanism, and the reason is a mechanism we would lose.** Our
stage gate routes ineligible candidates through OPUS so that a *protected floor can override the
curriculum gate* — S5's rule that floors are non-negotiable and outrank the stage. This run records
**70 `protected_floor_override` rescues**, every one of which is a stage-ineligible candidate
admitted because its lane is protected. A design that never draws ineligible shards cannot express
that rescue at all.

What we did take is the auditability half: `stage_eligibility` and `eligible_stages` are now derived
onto every shard manifest, so which stages may consume a shard is checkable from
`manifests/shards/` without running the scheduler.

*And it immediately re-taught F5.* The first version gated eligibility on the shard's **longest**
document fitting the stage window — which made agentic and indic eligible for **no stage at all**,
because one long document condemned the whole shard. That is precisely the failure F5 recorded,
where a single near-duplicate blocked every web shard and deleted 34% of the budget. The window
condition is now reported **per document** (`documents_fitting_window` / `documents_total`) and does
not gate the shard; length remains a per-document reserve, which is where it belongs.

### Still ahead of us, and staying that way

Corpus scale (122 shards vs 7) and a HuggingFace BPE tokenizer vs our hand-written one. The
assignment states *"The implementation may use a small corpus, tokenizer and model. The goal is not
scale."* Matching it would dilute the planted defects that make our controls non-vacuous, move every
measured number, and reduce the repeated exposure the trajectory report depends on — for a dimension
the rubric does not score.

## E13 — Independent adversarial audit, and the eight defects it found (2026-08-07)

Two independent auditors were given the spec and the repository but not our conclusions: one
adversarial code audit, one rubric scoring. Both converged on the same class of defect, and the
first proved it in the way that matters — by breaking the system and watching the evidence pass.

**Independent grade before fixes: 765/1000.** Heaviest losses: checkpoint/crash/resume/replay
84/150, mixture/floors/OPUS 96/150.

### C1 — `attention_crossings()` could not fail, and validated a mask the model never used

`build_attention_mask` set `mask[i][j] = 1` where `segment_ids[j] == segment_ids[i]`;
`attention_crossings` then counted entries where `segment_ids[j] != segment_ids[i]`. `(A == B) and
(A != B)` — unsatisfiable for every input, confirmed exhaustively over 4,096 segment layouts.

Worse, `build_attention_mask` was used by nothing in production: the model re-derived its own mask.
The auditor deleted segment isolation from the trainer — introducing genuine cross-document
attention leakage into training — and the pipeline reported **15/15 PASS, `verify_evidence` exit 0,
26/26 controls**. The single loudest claim in this submission was guarded by a check that could not
fail, one layer below the docstring congratulating itself for having fixed exactly that.

**Fixed.** `build_attention_mask` now delegates to `TinyLM.attention_mask` — the same call the
forward pass makes — and the counter compares against the segment ids independently. The control
mutates the model's mask to causal-only and requires the count to rise: measured **0 → 9 → 0**.

### C2 — Replay re-read the record it was checking

`_samples_from_record` built its sample list by iterating `entry["packed_sample_ids"]`, so
`sample_ids == entry["packed_sample_ids"]` compared a list to the list it was built from. The
auditor scrambled every pool cursor and shuffled every pool order; `all_match` stayed `True`. The
spec names this failure explicitly: *"if replay produces different batch hashes, replay fails"* — it
could not produce different hashes.

**Fixed.** Replay now restores the covering checkpoint, re-runs planner + OPUS + packer on a
dedicated branch, and compares regenerated ids/spans/hashes against the ledger. It re-trains,
because OPUS scores the live model and the parameter trajectory has to be reproduced or the
selection cannot be. New control rotates every lane's pool order and requires failure: **clean
`True`, rotated `False`, restored `True`.**

### C3 — `coverage()` counted its own enumeration

`duplicate_positions` and `missing_positions` were derived by enumerating `per_step` and comparing
against a second enumeration of the same `per_step`. Traces with a whole batch duplicated and with a
whole batch missing both scored 0/0. Now the stream is **reconstructed from the rank partition** and
compared to what was served; duplicated, missing and reordered batches are all detected.

### C4 — The crash was cosmetic

`crash()` appended a log line. The model, the checkpoint store and the planner cursors all survived —
`weight_hash` was bit-identical either side — and `restore()` read an in-memory dict. On-disk
checkpoints omitted the weights entirely, so nothing on the filesystem could have restarted anything.

**Fixed.** The crash randomises the weights, clears the in-memory checkpoint store and zeroes the
pool cursors; checkpoints are written with weights; the demo rescans the checkpoint directory and
reloads from disk. And the disk-fidelity claim is now made in the strongest available form: a
**subprocess** reconstructs the model from `ckpt_00024.json` alone and reproduces its recorded weight
hash.

### C5 — OPUS's score gate decided nothing

`below_proxy_threshold` fired **0 times in 296 judgements**. Every recorded rejection was
`stage_mismatch`, a difficulty-band string comparison that never reads the score. Structural: the
candidate pool was exactly the global batch, so `keep_n = round(8 × 0.82) = 7` and top-k could cut at
most one sample — a maximum expressible rejection of 12.5% against a plan asking for 18%.

**Fixed** in three parts: the planner oversamples 2× so there is a surplus to select from; the top-k
rank is computed over candidates that **survive the earlier gates** (ranking the raw pool let
eliminated candidates occupy slots above the cut); and unprotected lanes draw band-eligible samples,
while protected lanes deliberately still surface ineligible ones so the floor override retains its
reach past the stage gate. All six decision paths now fire: accepted 169, floor-override 191,
stage_mismatch 178, quota_full 100, reserved_for_anneal 57, **below_proxy_threshold 50**.

**And the fix produced a finding.** The plan assumes 18% rejection for supply sizing; this run
achieves **47.8%**. Recomputing feasibility at the measured rate moves **`reasoning` and `stem` into
breach** on top of agentic and indic. The two numbers used to sit pages apart, unconnected; the run
now reconciles them and writes `manifests/feasibility_at_observed_rejection.json`.

### C6 — Six hardcoded `[PASS]` lines, one of them printing on a failing run

Sixteen lines below a comment stating the principle, `run_demo.py` still emitted unconditional PASS
literals. The auditor deleted the anneal-reserve mechanism entirely and got
`[PASS] anneal_reserve_deferrals_recorded 0 deferrals over 0 documents`, and on a run that exited 1
the log still ended `[PASS] performance_measured`. All verdicts are now computed from observed

### C7 — A negative control silently never ran

`mask_validator_detects_loss_bearing_pad` sat inside `if pad_idx is not None`, and the sample it
probed had no padding — so it never executed, while `summarize()` reported

### C8 — Contaminated measurement populations, and packing claims that compared different events

`performance.json` summed all 38 raw steps — fork, control-fork, replay and ablation branches, plus
both executions of every crash-orphaned step — moving published packing utilization by 2 points. The
learning ledger had **no branch attribution at all**: 16 duplicate `(step, shard)` pairs, with the
pre-crash batches the consumption ledger correctly supersedes still counted.

Fixed: `TokenObservation` and shard exposures carry `branch_id` and `superseded`; `resume` mirrors

labelled. Measured after: **30 steps, one branch, zero duplicates, utilization reconstructs exactly.**

Packing: `long_context` and `structure_preserving` were byte-identical implementations, so "six

`structure_preserving` now packs complete samples without ever continuing one into a second window

makes it distinct from both `pad_only` and `best_fit`. And `documents_truncated` was comparing
different events per policy: both `concat_chop` and `best_fit` split over-length documents and only
one labelled it. Split and truncation are now reported separately — concat_chop **74 truncated / 31
split**, best_fit **0 / 15**. A test now fails if any two policies converge again.

### Also fixed

`window_tokens` double-counted padding (`raw_tokens` already includes it), inflating the published
figure ~10.7%. `dashboard.html` was committed, never regenerated by `run_demo`, and advertised a

### What survived the audit

Hash chain, resume's re-planning path, hand-written gradients (verified by a full-parameter

### The lesson worth keeping

### A definitional conflation that drove a graded conclusion

Binding the unconditional `[PASS]` literals to real predicates surfaced this. The feasibility check

a few hundred samples; falsified by `OVERSAMPLE` 2.0 → 6.0, which gives 45.6%, `|delta|=0.277`, FAIL.

`reasoning` flips to infeasible on a 0.6pp rate change. That is a property of the S5 plan's headroom

## E15 — Two gaps a sibling implementation still had on us (2026-08-09)

*Decision recorded before implementation; results appended below.*

The sibling implementation (`vedapragna-cs/era-v5-s6-data-ledger` at `32a0ab6` — its HEAD has since
been replaced with a copy of ours) was claimed to remain ahead on three axes: realistic training
path, per-role attribution, and throughput. Checked all three against both code bases.

**Throughput: not supportable.** Both report the same window / real / loss-bearing decomposition —
E12 records that we took it from them. They have a steady-state split excluding warm-up steps and a
`6·N·T` FLOPs estimate that we lack; their in-report `verify_against_ledger` is the same
reconstruction guarantee our `verify_evidence.py:127-142` provides from outside the report. We have
loader share of step time, `rank_compute_skew` across ranks, a six-policy packing bake-off and
`by_curriculum_stage`, which they lack. No blanket win either way.

**The other two are real, and this entry closes them.**

### Hypothesis

**H15a (per-role).** Role is currently spent once — `packing.py:153` turns it into a 0/1 `sup` bit
and discards it. `PackedSequence` has no role array and `TokenObservation` has none of its 20+ fields
carrying one. Therefore no aggregate in the system can separate `tool_call` loss from
`tool_observation` loss. Threading a per-position `role_codes` array through packing to the token
ledger should produce a `by_role` table whose token total equals the loss-bearing token total
exactly, and whose roles are all supervised roles.
*Refuted if* the by_role token total disagrees with `loss_bearing_tokens`, or if any context role
appears with a non-zero token count — either would mean the role array is misaligned with the mask
it is supposed to explain.

**H15b (optimizer).** `optimizer_state` in a checkpoint is currently the literal
`{"type": "sgd", "lr": 0.35}` and `scheduler_state` is `{"policy": "constant"}`. There is no state,
so "resume restores the optimizer" cannot fail and the claim is vacuous — the same defect class as
D25 and D27, one layer down. Replacing SGD with AdamW (warmup + cosine, global-norm clipping) makes
the moment buffers real state.
*Refuted if* dropping the restored moment buffers leaves the post-resume trajectory unchanged — that
would mean the buffers are not load-bearing and the drill still proves nothing.

Cost accepted in advance: AdamW adds two 72,256-element buffers per checkpoint, and every number
measured from a forward pass moves for the third time. Corrections table appended, per the rule that
historical measurements are superseded rather than edited.

### Results

**H15a (per-role) — supported.** Role codes ride every packed sequence beside the loss mask, through
all six packing policies, onto every token observation. Measured on run-a's surviving observations:
5,328 role-attributed tokens against 5,328 lane-attributed, and zero context roles carrying loss.

| depth | role | geometric-mean ppl | tokens |
|---|---|---|---|
| default | boundary / tool_call / text / assistant_plan | 37.82 / 121.97 / 190.78 / 336.32 | 41 / 24 / 5,215 / 48 |
| `--deep` | boundary / text / tool_call / assistant_plan | 7.07 / 41.35 / 70.76 / 95.52 | 211 / 26,187 / 114 / 228 |

At depth the two supervised agentic roles are the hardest things the model emits — `tool_call` 1.71x
and `assistant_plan` 2.31x plain text. That is the direction S5's 53% supervision-yield argument
assumed and had no measurement for. It is reported as a pointer, not a result: 114 and 228 tokens,
one lane, 1.3% exposure, and the same exposure confound that applies to lanes applies to roles.

**H15b (optimizer) — supported, and building the drill exposed a defect in the drill.** AdamW
(β=0.9/0.95, decoupled decay 0.01, warmup 6% + cosine to 10% of peak, global-norm clip 1.0). The
control trains past the checkpoint, restores twice, drops the moments on the second: weight hash at
restore identical either way, trajectories diverge after three steps.

*The first version of that control passed with `load_optimizer_state` deleted from `restore()`.* It
restored immediately after saving, so the live buffers were already the checkpoint's and the
comparison never involved the checkpoint's copy — it proved the moments matter, not that resume
restores them. Fixed by training past the checkpoint first and asserting
`model.optimizer_hash == ck["optimizer_hash"]`. The same lesson as C1/C2/C7: a control that never
observes the state it claims to check will pass on a build that has deleted the mechanism.

**Divergence, and the sweep that fixed it.** The SGD rates (0.35 / 1.2) diverged under Adam to a mean
loss of 14.3 nats against a uniform baseline of ln(512) = 6.24, because Adam's per-parameter step is
~lr whatever the gradient scale. Nothing in the pipeline gated on loss magnitude, so the run still
reported 15/15. Swept at default depth: 3e-3 → 5.951, 6e-3 → 5.703, 1e-2 → 5.668, **2e-2 → 5.491**,
4e-2 → 6.020 (lowest minimum of the sweep at 3.348, then climbing back above 6 — overshoot, not
learning). Defaults: 2e-2, and 1e-2 at `--deep`.

**A second finding from the test, and it is a property of Adam not of this code.** A probe using a
*constant* gradient reports "the moments are not load-bearing" and is wrong: `m̂/√v̂` converges to
`sign(g)` regardless of history, and bias correction makes a fresh optimizer's first step equal to it
exactly. The test uses a varying gradient sequence and records why.
